# Brief for drafting one study day of Yaoki N2, unit 3 (原因・理由, cause and reason)

You write ONE study day as a JSON object, in exactly the shape of `model-day4.json` in this folder (read it first, and `../../README.md` for the field rules and the markup). Write the result to the file named in your task, as a single JSON object (the day itself, not wrapped in anything). Do not edit any other file.

## The app and the learner
Yaoki is a JLPT N2 study app. The learner is an adult preparing for N2 (exam on 6 December 2026). Explanations are in English, short and plain. Japanese is natural, standard, polite or plain as the situation needs, and at N2 level: vocabulary and kanji a learner meets at N2 or below. Each study day teaches two grammar points of the unit, then a comparison, a drill, three exam-style questions, a kanji-and-words scene, and one reading or listening lesson.

## Hard rules (a native-speaking teacher will review; mistakes here cost their limited time)
1. **Every item has exactly one right answer.** Every wrong option must be indefensible to a native speaker in that sentence. If a distractor could be accepted by some speakers, replace it. Check every star (文の文法2) item: the four tiles must fit in only one order; check each tile against every other slot.
2. **No false rules.** A reason line must not teach a rule that is not true in general. If a form is wrong only in this sentence, say why in this sentence.
3. **All sentences are original.** Do not copy from Shin Kanzen Master, Nihongo So-Matome, other textbooks or past JLPT papers.
4. **Natural Japanese.** Sentences a native speaker would actually say or write. Avoid odd collocations.
5. **Level.** Furigana with [漢字|かな] only on words above N2 in teach and drill text, very sparingly. None in exam-style items, the scene check, or reading passages and questions.
6. **Reasons for every option.** Choice items in the drill have `why` with one line per option (the right one included). Exam-style items have one `explain` that covers the right answer and why the others fail.
7. **The glosses stay fixed:** つぎへ Next, こたえる Check etc. are handled by the app; you only write content.
8. The `title` must be exactly the syllabus title given in your task. Item ids follow the model: `u03dN.p1`, `u03dN.p2` (points), `u03dN.c1` (compare check), `u03dN.d1`…`d6` (drill), `u03dN.e1`…`e3` (exam-style), `u03dN.k1` (scene check), `u03dN.r1` (reading or listening item).

## What a day contains
- `grammar.points`: the two points of the day. For each: `form`, `gloss` (a few English words), `connect` (2 to 3 rows; mark the form with {…}), two `examples` (Japanese with the form in {…}, and an English translation; no `clip` field, the app adds sound later), and `watch` (a note on the typical mistake, one wrong sentence `bad` and its corrected sentence `good`, plain text, no markup).
- `grammar.compare`: `intro` (one sentence), three `forms` (the two new points and one from an earlier day of this unit or a common confusion; `mark` is down for a bad result, up for "too much", dot for neutral or "all the more"; `tag` = short Japanese label · English gloss), and a `check` choice item with three options.
- `grammar.drill`: six items: four choice items (文の文法1, four options) and two star items (文の文法2), at least two items on each of the day's points. Use ^…^ to mark the clue in a choice sentence (the words that decide the answer). Star items need `hint`, `full`, `explain`, and a `bank` order that is not already correct.
- `grammar.exam`: three items in strict exam format: two 文の文法1 (four options, `explain`) and one 文の文法2 star item.
- `scene`: a real place or situation where an N2 learner meets 8 written words (signs, forms, notices, a screen). `ja`, `en`, `intro` (one English sentence telling the learner what to tap), and 8 `words` each with `id` (short English), `word`, `reading`, `gloss`, `parts` (two parts: [part, reading in kana (katakana for on'yomi of a single kanji), short meaning]). Then a `check` choice item in an N2 vocabulary format: 漢字読み (q with the _word_ underlined, four readings), 表記 (q with the _kana word_ underlined, four kanji spellings), 語形成 (prefix or suffix, （　　）), or 文脈規定 (（　　）, four words). Use one of the day's 8 words. `explain` gives the reason.
- `skill`: as specified in your task (reading or listening).
  - Reading: `kind: "reading"`, `ja`/`en` the skill name, `ask` (the English question the skill trains, e.g. "Why did the writer change their mind?"), `seconds` (60 to 120), `passage` (150 to 250 characters, original, N2 level, with ^…^ around the signposts that the explanation points to), `strategy` (one English sentence: what to look for), and `item`: a choice item with type `読解・<skill>`, `q` the Japanese question, four `options`, `short` labels, `answer`, `explain`, and `why` (one line per option; the right one may be "").
  - Listening: `kind: "listening"`, `ja`/`en` (the item type: 即時応答 Quick response, ポイント理解 Point comprehension or 課題理解 Task comprehension), `ask` (the Japanese instruction shown on screen, e.g. いちばんいい返事は？ or 男の人はどうして遅れましたか。), and `item`: a choice item with `type` the item type, `q` (for 即時応答 the line itself, e.g. 男：…; otherwise the question), `script` (every spoken line as an array of strings, each starting 男： 女： or ナレーター：), `options` (3 spoken replies for 即時応答; 4 printed choices otherwise), `printed` (true for ポイント理解 and 課題理解), `short`, `answer`, `why` (one line per option). Also `audio` at the skill level: the exact lines to synthesise, as [speaker, text, respelling-or-null] with speaker N (narrator), M (man) or F (woman). For 即時応答: the line, then N "1", the reply, N "2", the reply, N "3", the reply. For the others: N reads the situation and the question, then the conversation, then N reads the question again. Use a respelling (kana) only where a voice could misread (numbers, names, 何 = なに/なん).

## Style of the English
Plain, short sentences. No em-dashes. Explain with the sentence in front of the learner ("〜あまり follows a feeling noun with の, and 心配 is one"). British or American spelling consistently (the app uses "colour"? No: follow the model, which uses plain international English).

## Before you finish
Re-read every item as a strict examiner. For each choice item, try each wrong option in the sentence and confirm it fails for the reason you gave. For each star item, try other orders. Make sure every sentence in `full` equals before + tiles + after exactly, character for character. Then validate your JSON (python3 -c "import json;json.load(open('FILE'))").
At the end, report in under 150 words: anything you were unsure about (an item that is a judgement call on naturalness, a word whose level is unclear), so it can be flagged amber for the reviewer.
