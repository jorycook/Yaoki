# Brief for drafting one study day of Yaoki N2

You write ONE study day as a JSON object, in exactly the shape of `../../n2/drafts/model-day4.json` (an N2 day; read it first for the shape, and `../../README.md` for the field rules and the markup). The level is different, so the rules below replace the N2 ones wherever they differ. Write the result to the file named in your task, as a single JSON object (the day itself, not wrapped in anything). Do not edit any other file.

## The app and the learner
Yaoki is a JLPT study app. This is the **N2** course: the learner has finished the N3 course (`../../n3/units.json` lists what they have been taught) and is preparing for N2 (exam on 6 December 2026). Explanations are in English: short, plain, friendly; ordinary grammar words are fine (te-form, plain form, passive, noun, clause). No romaji anywhere. Each study day teaches two grammar points, then a comparison, a drill, three exam-style questions, a kanji-and-words scene, and one reading or listening lesson.

## Hard rules (a native-speaking teacher will review; mistakes here cost their limited time)
1. **Every item has exactly one right answer.** Every wrong option must be clearly wrong to a native speaker in that sentence. Check every star (文の文法2) item: the four tiles must fit in only one order; check each tile against every other slot.
2. **No false rules.** A reason line must not teach a rule that is not true in general. If a form is wrong only in this sentence, say why in this sentence.
3. **All sentences are original.** Do not copy from Genki, Minna no Nihongo, other textbooks or past JLPT papers.
4. **Natural Japanese.** Sentences a native speaker would actually say. Polite です／ます style unless the point needs plain forms.
5. **Level.** N2 grammar and vocabulary and below (all N3 and lower grammar may be used freely). Lean on the day's points and earlier units of the syllabus (`../units.json`); a point from a later N2 unit only where unavoidable (say so in the report). Sentences natural, most under 40 characters. Adult, everyday topics: work, study, neighbours, shops, travel, health, news.
6. **Kanji and furigana.** Write Japanese as an educated adult would, with the kanji of everyday writing (no list is enforced at N2). In **teach, compare and drill text**, give a reading `[漢字|かんじ]` only on words above N2 or rare kanji, sparingly. In **exam-style items, the scene check, reading passages and questions, and listening printed choices**, no readings, as the real paper does. Never put a reading on the kanji a 漢字読み item asks about.
7. **Reasons for every option.** Choice items in the drill have `why` with one line per option (the right one included). Exam-style items have one `explain` that covers the right answer and why the others fail.
8. **Ids** follow the model, with no level prefix at N2: `u01dN.p1`, `u01dN.p2` (points), `u01dN.c1` (compare check), `u01dN.d1`…`d6` (drill), `u01dN.e1`…`e3` (exam-style), `u01dN.k1` (scene check), `u01dN.r1` (reading or listening item). Replace the unit number with the one in your task (two digits). Replace the unit number with the one in your task.
9. The `title` must be exactly the syllabus title given in your task.

## Spacing and labels
- No spaces between phrases anywhere: the N2 paper is written as normal Japanese text.
- Connect rules write a noun slot as 〜 (never めいし or 名詞); the English `pos` column says what 〜 is.
- Listening item types are written plainly: 発話表現, 即時応答, ポイント理解, 課題理解.

## What a day contains
- `grammar.points`: the two points. For each: `id`, `form`, `gloss` (a few English words), `connect` (2 to 3 rows: `pos` in plain English such as "Noun" or "Verb, ます form"; `rule` in Japanese with the form in {…}), two `examples` (Japanese with the form in {…} and readings on kanji, plus an English translation; no `clip`), and `watch` (the typical beginner mistake: `note`, one wrong sentence `bad` and its corrected sentence `good`, plain text without markup or readings).
- `grammar.compare`: `intro` (one sentence), three `forms` (the two new points and one earlier point or a common confusion; `mark`: dot for neutral, down for a negative meaning, up for "more"; `tag` = short Japanese label · English gloss), and a `check` choice item with three options.
- `grammar.drill`: six items: four choice items (文の文法1, four options) and two star items (文の文法2), at least two on each point. Use ^…^ to mark the clue in a choice sentence. Star items need `hint`, `full`, `explain`, `star` (0 to 3) and a `bank` order (indexes 0 to 3) that is not already 0,1,2,3.
- `grammar.exam`: three items in strict exam format: two 文の文法1 (four options, `explain`) and one 文の文法2 star item. No readings.
- `scene`: a real place or situation where a beginner meets 8 written words (a sign, a menu, a form, a timetable). `ja`, `en`, `intro` (one English sentence telling the learner what to tap), and 8 `words`, each with `id` (short English), `word` (written as on the sign), `reading`, `gloss`, `parts`: one entry per kanji, as [kanji, reading of that kanji in this word in kana (katakana for on'yomi), short meaning]; a word with okurigana lists its kanji only. Mostly N2-level words (the level being learnt). Then a `check` choice item in an N2 vocabulary format (語形成, a prefix or suffix in （　　）, is also allowed), using one of the day's words: 漢字読み (q with the _word_ underlined, four kana readings), 表記 (q with the _kana word_ underlined, four kanji spellings), 文脈規定 (（　　）, four words), 言い換え類義 (q with a _phrase_ underlined; four sentences, the one closest in meaning is right), or 用法 (q is the word itself; four sentences using it, only one correctly). `explain` gives the reason.
- `skill`: as given in your task.
  - Reading: `kind: "reading"`, `ja`/`en` the skill (内容理解（短文） Short passage, 内容理解（中文） Mid-length passage, 情報検索 Finding information), `ask` (the English question the skill trains), `seconds` (60 to 120), `passage` (200 to 350 characters, original, N2, written as the paper writes it, with ^…^ around the words the explanation points to), `strategy` (one English sentence: what to look for), and `item`: a choice item with type `読解・<skill>`, `q` the Japanese question, four `options`, `short`, `answer`, `explain`, and `why` (one line per option; the right one may be "").
  - Listening: `kind: "listening"`, `ja`/`en` (the item type), `ask` (the Japanese instruction shown on screen), and `item`: a choice item with `type`, `q`, `script` (every spoken line as an array of strings starting 男： 女： or ナレーター：), `options`, `printed`, `short`, `answer`, `why`. Also `audio` at the skill level: the exact lines to synthesise, as [speaker, text, respelling-or-null] with speaker N (narrator), M (man) or F (woman). Write audio text in normal Japanese; give a kana respelling wherever a voice could misread (numbers, counters, times, 何, names).
    - 発話表現 Expressions: on the paper a picture shows a situation. Here `situation` (at the skill level) is one English sentence describing the picture, `ask` is the Japanese question (e.g. 友だちに　ペンを　かりたいです。何と　いいますか。), `q` is the same question, three spoken `options` (not printed: `printed: false`), and `audio`: N the question, then N "1", the option, N "2", the option, N "3", the option.
    - 即時応答 Quick response: `q` is the line itself (男：…), three spoken replies, `printed: false`; audio: the line, then N "1", reply, N "2", reply, N "3", reply.
    - ポイント理解 / 課題理解: four printed choices (`printed: true`); audio: N reads the situation and the question, then the conversation, then N reads the question again.
    - 概要理解 Gist: nothing printed (`printed: false`), four spoken `options`. N reads only the situation (no question yet), then one speaker talks (a short talk, message or radio piece, about 40 seconds), then N reads the question, then N "1", option, N "2", option, N "3", option, N "4", option. The question asks what the talk is mainly about or what the speaker thinks; the options are short. `ask` is the Japanese instruction for the type, `q` the question.

## Style of the English
Plain, short sentences. No em-dashes. Explain with the sentence in front of the learner ("も replaces は here because the second person is a student too").

## Before you finish
Re-read every item as a strict examiner. For each choice item, try each wrong option in the sentence and confirm it fails for the reason you gave. For each star item, try other orders. Make sure every `full` equals before + tiles + after exactly, character for character (readings in `full` are allowed; they are removed before comparing). Check every kanji against the list in rule 6.
Keep each helper script in a folder of your own (mktemp -d), never in a shared scratch folder. Within a day, no two items may share a scenario or sentence frame (a drill item and an exam item about the same hair dryer, the same 見るたびに…思い出す), no sentence may reappear in the passage, and the same answer phrase (e.g. 一日おきに) should not be the key more than twice. Spread the right answers over positions 1 to 4 (the real paper does; drafts tend to put them first). Options must differ once readings are removed (write in kana where only the reading would differ). Star items: no tile ends with 。, and do not split a word across tiles; the ★ must not fall on a tile that is obvious on its own. Then validate your JSON (python3 -c "import json;json.load(open('FILE'))").
At the end, report in under 150 words: anything you were unsure about (naturalness, level, a word you were not sure is N2 or below), so it can be flagged amber for the reviewer.

## Review of the level below (decided 15 October 2026)
Some overlap with what the learner met at N3 is wanted, as review, not as filler.
- A day whose title ends in （復習） teaches a point the N3 course already taught. Teach it as review at N2 depth: say in one line that it was met at N3, then go further (written or formal uses, harder joins, the near-synonyms N2 tests against it). Its compare puts it next to the N2 points it is confused with.
- On other days, the compare's third form may be a related point from the N3 course (for example 〜からといって next to N3 〜といっても) where that is the real confusion a learner has.
- The まとめ day: one drill item and one exam item set a point of this unit against a related N3 point.
